A principle, not a slogan

Every click
should earn
its place.

Interaction isn't decoration. Every decision, click and piece of feedback has a learning purpose — or it doesn't belong in the experience.

I have a slightly unreasonable dislike of clicking "Next" when nothing happened.

If an interaction doesn't help someone think, decide, practice or remember, I don't think it belongs.

Make it real.

Learners understand why knowledge matters because they're using it in contexts that resemble their actual work.

Make them think.

Learning involves decisions and problem-solving — not passive scrolling through slides.

Make it count.

Every click, animation and piece of feedback serves the learning goal, or it gets cut.

05

Why this approach

Every interaction has a purpose.

No clicks for the sake of clicks. Every action a learner takes contributes to the learning itself.

Practice the real world.

Learners face constraints, make decisions and see consequences — before those decisions matter in reality.

Designed with evidence.

Strategies are grounded in learning science and evaluation, rather than borrowed from trends.

06

How I work

  1. 01

    Understand

    The learner, the context, the problem and the change we're actually trying to make happen.

  2. 02

    Design

    Turn the problem into an experience — a scenario, a simulation, a piece of interactive learning.

  3. 03

    Prototype

    Test the interaction before investing in full production. Cheap to change, early.

  4. 04

    Build

    Create, integrate and refine the final learning experience.

  5. 05

    Evaluate

    Find out whether the experience actually worked — and feed that back in.
